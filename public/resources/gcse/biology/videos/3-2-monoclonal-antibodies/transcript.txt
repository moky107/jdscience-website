Hello and welcome back to JD Science. Today, we are exploring monoclonal antibodies. These are identical antibodies made from a single parent cell. A mouse is injected with an antigen and its B lymphocytes produce antibodies against it. These lymphocytes are fused with tumor cells to make a hybridoma. The hybridoma divides rapidly and produces large amounts of identical antibodies. They can be used to locate molecules in cells, for pregnancy tests, and for testing hormone levels. In cancer treatment, they bind to cancer cells and tag them for attack by white blood cells. Compared with chemotherapy and radiotherapy, they target cells more precisely and have fewer side effects. A monoclonal antibody recognises a particular antigen or part of an antigen. In traditional hybridoma production, an antibody producing B lymphocyte is fused with a tumor cell to combine specificity with the ability to divide repeatedly. Monoclonal antibodies can be used in pregnancy tests, diagnostics and some targeted cancer treatments. They are not automatically free from side effects.
