Hello and welcome back to JD Science. Today we are exploring respiration. Respiration releases energy from glucose. Aerobic respiration uses oxygen and gives carbon dioxide and water. It takes place in the mitochondria. When oxygen is in short supply, anaerobic respiration takes over. It releases much less energy than aerobic respiration and produces lactic acid in animals. Lactic acid builds up and causes muscle fatigue and an oxygen debt. Deep breathing after exercise repays this debt. In yeast and plants, anaerobic respiration produces ethanol and carbon dioxide. This is called fermentation and it is used to make bread and alcoholic drinks. During exercise, heart rate and breathing rate increase. Respiration is a chemical process in cells that transfers energy for life processes. Breathing is the physical re movement of air into and out of the lungs. In aerobic respiration, glucose reacts with oxygen to produce carbon dioxide and water. During intense exercise, anaerobic respiration in muscle cells produces lactate and releases less energy per glucose molecule. Oxygen is needed afterwards for recovery, but lactate is not simply the direct cause of all muscle fatigue. As you revise this topic, try to explain the key process in your own words.
