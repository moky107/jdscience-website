Hello and welcome back to JD science. Today we are exploring how substances move in and out of cells. In diffusion particles move from high concentration to low concentration along a concentration gradient. It is faster when the temperature is higher, the concentration gradient is steeper or the surface area is larger. Oxygen diffuses into the blood in the lungs and carbon dioxide diffuses out. Alveoli have a large surface area, a thin lining, a good blood supply and ventilation. Osmosis is the diffusion of water across a partially permeable membrane. Active transport moves substances against a concentration gradient using energy from respiration. Root hair cells use active transport to absorb mineral ions. Diffusion and osmosis are passive processes. They do not require the cell to supply metabolic energy. Active transport is different because carrier proteins move substances against their concentration gradient using energy, often supplied by ATP. A useful comparison is oxygen entering cells by diffusion, water crossing a partially permeable membrane by osmosis and mineral ions entering root hair cells by active transport.
