## Page 5

A12.2.1 The rate relationship

 • Average rate = change in amount of reactant used or product formed ÷ time
 taken.
 • Use the change in amount over the chosen time interval.
 • Rate is not just 'amount ÷ time' unless the amount starts from zero or the
 question explicitly gives the amount changed.
 • Always include the correct compound unit.

## Page 6

What can 'amount' mean?

 • The specification allows rate to be calculated from reactant or product data.
 • Examples: mass of reactant used, mass of product formed, volume of gas
 formed, or amount in moles.
 • Use the quantity and units given in the question.
 • Do not convert units unless the question requires it or the units are
 inconsistent.

## Page 7

Units for rate

 • If amount is in g and time is in s → rate in g s⁻¹.
 • If gas volume is in cm³ and time is in s → rate in cm³ s⁻¹.
 • If amount is in mol and time is in s → rate in mol s⁻¹.
 • If time is in min → the rate is per minute unless you convert time to seconds.

## Page 8

Calculation method

 • Step 1: identify the start and end values.
 • Step 2: calculate the change in amount.
 • Step 3: identify the time interval.
 • Step 4: rate = change in amount ÷ time interval.
 • Step 5: give the answer with appropriate units and sensible rounding.

## Page 9

Worked example 1: product formed

 • A reaction produces 72 cm³ of gas in 24 s.
 • Change in product = 72 cm³.
 • Time = 24 s.
 • Average rate = 72 ÷ 24 = 3.0 cm³ s⁻¹.
 • Interpretation: on average, 3.0 cm³ of gas is formed each second over this
 interval.

## Page 12

Worked example 2: reactant used

 • A solid reactant decreases in mass from 8.40 g to 6.90 g in 50 s.
 • Change in reactant amount = 8.40 − 6.90 = 1.50 g.
 • Average rate of reactant use = 1.50 ÷ 50.
 • Average rate = 0.030 g s⁻¹.
 • The subtraction step is essential because the initial amount was not zero.

## Page 15

Time conversions

 • Check whether time values use the same unit.
 • 1 minute = 60 seconds.
 • Example: 120 cm³ formed in 2.0 min.
 • Using minutes: rate = 120 ÷ 2.0 = 60 cm³ min⁻¹.
 • Using seconds: 2.0 min = 120 s; rate = 120 ÷ 120 = 1.0 cm³ s⁻¹.

## Page 16

Worked example 3: interval from a table

 • At 20 s, gas volume = 18 cm³. At 50 s, gas volume = 42 cm³.
 • Change in product = 42 − 18 = 24 cm³.
 • Time interval = 50 − 20 = 30 s.
 • Average rate from 20–50 s = 24 ÷ 30 = 0.80 cm³ s⁻¹.

Reading a graph

 • To calculate average rate from a graph, read the amount at two stated times.
 • Calculate the vertical change: final amount − initial amount.
 • Calculate the horizontal change: final time − initial time.
 • Average rate = vertical change ÷ horizontal change.
 • This is the gradient of the straight line joining the two selected points.

## Page 21

Reactant graphs: direction matters

 • A reactant amount usually decreases as the reaction proceeds.
 • For rate of reactant use, calculate the amount used as a positive change: initial
 amount − final amount.
 • Example: 5.0 g falls to 3.8 g in 40 s → 1.2 g used.
 • Average rate of use = 1.2 ÷ 40 = 0.030 g s⁻¹.

Error Detective: answers

## Page 28

Comparing rates

 • Rates can only be compared directly when their units are compatible.
 • A larger numerical rate means a greater amount changes per unit time when
 the same units are used.
 • Convert units first if necessary.
 • Use calculated values as evidence rather than relying only on visual
 impressions.

Exam style application: answers


    • (a) 96 ÷ 32 = 3.0 cm³ s⁻¹.
    • (b) Change = 6.80 − 5.30 = 1.50 g; rate = 1.50 ÷ 75 = 0.020 g s⁻¹.
    • (c) Change in product = 39 − 15 = 24 cm³; time interval = 60 − 20 = 40 s; rate = 24
   ÷ 40 = 0.60 cm³ s⁻¹.
    • (d) The unit identifies the amount measured and the time basis, so the numerical
   value can be interpreted correctly.
