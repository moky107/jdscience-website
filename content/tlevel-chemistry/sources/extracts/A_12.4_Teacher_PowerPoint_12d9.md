    • At the same temperature, a greater proportion of collisions can have sufficient

## Page 5

A12.4.1 Why rate matters in industry

 • Industrial processes are designed to make useful products efficiently and
 consistently.
 • If a reaction proceeds faster, a production batch can reach its required
 endpoint sooner.
 • A shorter reaction stage can reduce turnaround time.
 • Faster turnaround can allow equipment to be used for more production cycles
 in the same period.

## Page 6

From faster rate to lower cost

 • Catalyst → increased reaction rate.
 • Increased rate → shorter time needed for the reaction stage.
 • Shorter reaction time → faster turnaround of the reactor or production line.
 • Faster turnaround can reduce operating costs per batch and increase
 production capacity.
 • The specification requires this rate–turnaround–cost link.

## Page 7

Important qualification: costs

 • A catalyst can reduce costs because faster reactions reduce turnaround time.
 • Real industrial cost depends on the whole process, including catalyst purchase,
 recovery/replacement, energy, maintenance and separation.
 • For A12.4.1, the required advantage is: increased rate → faster turnaround → reduced
 costs.
 • Do not claim that every catalyst makes every part of a process cheaper.

## Page 11

A12.4.2 Activation energy

 • Activation energy, Ea, is the minimum energy required for a reaction to occur.
 • A catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway.
 • The alternative pathway has a lower activation energy.
 • At the same temperature, more collisions can have enough energy to
 overcome the lower barrier.
 • The reaction rate therefore increases.

## Page 13

Linking molecular explanation to industrial
advantage
 • Lower Ea does not mean the reactant particles have gained extra kinetic
 energy.
 • The energy barrier is lower because the reaction follows an alternative
 pathway.
 • More successful collisions per unit time → faster reaction.
 • Faster reaction → shorter production time → faster turnaround → potential
 cost reduction.

## Page 14

What the catalyst does not change

 • A catalyst does not change the enthalpy level of the reactants.
 • A catalyst does not change the enthalpy level of the products.
 • A catalyst therefore does not change ΔH.
 • It changes the pathway and activation energy, not the overall enthalpy change.

Production data challenge: answer


    • Production capacity increases from 12 to 18 batches per week in this scenario.
    • The stated reaction-stage operating cost falls by £200 per batch.
    • A lower-Ea pathway increases reaction rate, allowing faster turnaround.
    • The numerical data support the stated advantages for this scenario; they should
   not be generalised to every industrial process.

## Page 21

Common misconceptions

 • ‘A catalyst gives particles more energy.’ Incorrect: it lowers the activation-energy
 barrier.
 • ‘A catalyst changes ΔH.’ Incorrect: reactant and product enthalpy levels are unchanged.
 • ‘A catalyst is used up to make the reaction faster.’ Incorrect: it is not permanently
 chemically changed overall.
 • ‘A faster reaction automatically means zero cost.’ Incorrect: the specification links
 faster turnaround with reduced costs, but industrial processes still have costs.

Exam style answers

    • (c) 10 − 4 = 6 h.
    • (d) Reactant enthalpy is unchanged (1) and product enthalpy is unchanged, so
   their enthalpy difference, ΔH, is unchanged (1).
