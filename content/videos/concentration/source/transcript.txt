[30.10–35.96] What is concentration? Concentration tells us how much solute is dissolved in a given
[35.96–42.14] volume of solution. A solute is the substance being dissolved, while a solvent is the liquid
[42.14–48.30] that dissolves it. For example, when sodium chloride dissolves in water, sodium chloride
[48.30–52.90] is the solute and water is the solvent. The resulting mixture is
[52.90–57.40] is called a solution. A concentrated solution contains more dissolved
[57.40–63.02] solute per unit volume than a dilute solution. Concentration can be expressed in different
[63.02–68.96] units depending on the information provided. Calculating concentration in grams per cubic
[68.96–74.94] decimeter. When concentration is expressed in grams per cubic decimeter, we use the following
[75.44–81.82] formula. Concentration equals mass of solute divided by volume of solution. The mass must
[81.82–87.92] be in grams and the volume must be in cubic decimeters. Remember that 1 cubic decimeter
[87.92–94.34] is equal to 1000 cubic centimeters. Therefore, if the volume is given in cubic centimeters,
[94.94–98.20] we must divide it by 1000 before using the formula.
[98.88–105.16] Let's look at an example. Worked example 1. A student dissolves 10 grams of sodium chloride
[105.16–112.40] in water to make 250 cubic centimeters of solution. Calculate the concentration in grams per cubic
[112.40–119.76] decimeter. First, convert convert the volume into cubic decimeters. 250 divided by 1000
[119.76–128.32] gives 0.25 cubic decimeters. Next, divide the mass by the volume. 10 divided by 0.25
[128.32–135.02] gives 40. Therefore, the concentration is 40 grams per cubic decimeter. Notice that we
[135.02–140.62] use the final volume of the solution, not simply the volume of water added. Calculating
[140.62–146.84] concentration in moles per cubic decimeter. Sometimes, concentration is expressed in moles
[146.84–148.98] per cubic decimeter rather than
[148.98–155.70] grams per cubic decimeter. In this case, we use concentration equals number of moles divided
[155.70–162.58] by volume in cubic decimeters. Let's apply this formula. Worked example 2. A solution
[162.58–170.08] contains 0.5 moles of sodium hydroxide in 250 cubic centimeters of solution. Calculate
[170.08–178.26] its concentration in moles per cubic decimeter. First, convert 250 cubic centimeters into cubic
[178.26–186.68] decimeters. This gives 0.25 cubic decimeters. Next, divide the number of moles by the volume.
[187.28–195.90] 0.5 divided by 0.25 gives 2. Therefore, the concentration is 2 moles per cubic decimeter.
