[30.32–39.18] Let's begin. What is a mole? Atoms, molecules and ions are extremely small particles. Because
[39.18–45.66] of their small size, chemists cannot conveniently count them individually. Instead, we use a
[45.66–55.30] unit called the mole. One mole contains approximately 6.02 x 10 to the power of 23 particles. This
[55.30–62.10] number is known as the Avogadro constant. For example, one mole of carbon atoms contains
[62.10–70.44] approximately 6.02 x 10 to the power of 23 carbon atoms. Similarly, one mole of water
[70.44–76.76] molecules contains the same number of water molecules. However, one mole of carbon and
[76.76–82.90] one mole of water do not have the same mass. Their masses depend on their relative atomic
[82.90–90.68] or formula masses. What is molar mass? Molar mass is the mass of one mole of a substance.
[91.24–98.02] It is measured in grams per mole. For example, the relative atomic mass of magnesium is 24.
[98.54–106.18] Therefore, one mole of magnesium has a mass of approximately 24 grams. Water has the chemical
[106.98–114.02] formula H2O. Its relative formula mass is calculated by adding two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen
[114.02–122.46] atom. Two multiplied by one plus 16 gives 18. Therefore, one mole of water has a mass
[122.46–129.70] of 18 grams. How do we calculate moles? To calculate the number of moles, we use the
[129.70–137.24] formula. Number of moles equals mass divided by molar mass. The mass must be in grams while
[137.24–142.50] molar mass is measured in grams per mole. Let's apply this formula to an example.
[143.64–149.86] Worked example 1. Calculate the number of moles in 12 grams of magnesium. The relative
[149.86–156.82] atomic mass of magnesium is 24. First, write down the formula. Number of magnesium equals
[156.82–165.34] mass divided by molar mass. Next, substitute the values. 12 divided by 24 gives 0.5
[165.34–172.40] Therefore, 12 grams of magnesium contains 0.5 moles. This means we have half a mole
[172.40–178.86] of magnesium atoms. Worked example 2. Calculating mass. Sometimes
[178.86–184.56] we are given the number of moles and asked to calculate the mass. In this case, we rearrange
[184.56–192.04] the formula. Mass equals number of moles multiplied by molar mass. For example, calculate the
[192.04–199.24] mass of two moles of carbon dioxide. Carbon dioxide has the formula CO2. The relative
[199.24–206.10] atomic mass of carbon is 12 while oxygen is 16. Therefore, the relative formula mass
[206.10–214.94] of carbon is 12 plus 2 multiplied by 16. This gives 44. Now, multiply the number of
[214.94–222.50] moles by the molar mass. 2 multiplied by 44 gives 88. Therefore, the mass of two moles
[222.50–228.18] of carbon dioxide is 88 grams. Practice question. Now it's your turn.
