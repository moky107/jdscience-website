Hello and welcome back to JD science. Today we are exploring plant tissues and organs. A leaf has several tissues. The epidermis covers the leaf. The palisade mesophyll is packed with chloroplasts for photosynthesis. The spongy mesophyll has air spaces for gas exchange. Xylem and phloem form the veins. Guard cells control the stomata which control gas exchange and water loss. Water evaporates through the stomata which pulls water up the xylem. This is transpiration. Transpiration is faster in warm, dry, windy conditions and in bright light. Xylem carries water and mineral ions upwards. Phloem carries sugars up and down the plant. Transpiration is the loss of water vapour from leaves, mainly through stomata. Water evaporates from mesophyll cell surfaces and diffuses into the air. This creates tension that helps draw a continuous column of water up the xylem. Phloem transports dissolved sugars from sources such as photosynthesising leaves to sinks such as roots and growing fruits. The direction depends on the location of sources and sinks.
