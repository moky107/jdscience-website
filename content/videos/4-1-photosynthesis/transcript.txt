Hello and welcome back to JD Science. Today we are exploring photosynthesis. Plants use carbon dioxide and water with energy from sunlight and chlorophyll to make glucose and oxygen. Carbon dioxide enters through the stomata. Water travels up from the roots. The equation is 6 carbon dioxide plus 6 water gives glucose plus 6 oxygen. Glucose can be respiration converted into starch for storage made into cellulose for cell walls combined with nitrates to make amino acids or converted into lipids for seeds. The rate of photosynthesis depends on temperature, intensity and carbon dioxide concentration. Enzymes can become denatured at high temperatures. Light intensity falls off with distance by the inverse square law. Doubling the distance cuts the intensity to a quarter. The balanced symbol equation is 6 carbon dioxide molecules plus 6 water molecules producing 1 molecule and 6 oxygen molecules using light energy. A limiting factor is the condition in shortest supply relative to the plant's needs. Increasing light will not keep increasing the rate if carbon dioxide or temperature can be treated as a point source and other conditions are controlled.
