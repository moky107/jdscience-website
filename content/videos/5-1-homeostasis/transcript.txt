Temperature, ion content, water content and blood glucose are all controlled. Control systems have three parts. Receptors detect a. A change, a coordinator such as the brain processes the information and effectors such as muscles and glands produce the response. Negative feedback means the output of a process controls its own level. For example, when blood glucose rises, insulin is released to lower it. When glucose falls, glucagon is released higher tier. Negative feedback opposes a change from a normal operating range. For example, after a meal, rising blood glucose stimulates pancreatic beta cells to release insulin, promoting glucose uptake and storage. When blood glucose falls, pancreatic alpha cells release glucagon, stimulating the liver to release glucose.
