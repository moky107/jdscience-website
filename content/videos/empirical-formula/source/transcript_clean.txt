[28.48–30.32] What is an empirical formula?
[30.94–36.84] An empirical formula shows the simplest whole number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound.
[37.46–42.36] For example, glucose has the molecular formula C6H12O6.
[43.32–50.48] This means that one molecule contains six carbon atoms, twelve hydrogen atoms, and six oxygen atoms.
[50.86–56.68] If we divide all these numbers by six we obtain a ratio of one to two to one.
[57.08–61.50] Therefore the empirical formula of glucose is CH2O.
[62.04–70.36] Notice that an empirical formula shows the simplest ratio of atoms, rather than necessarily showing the actual number of atoms in a molecule.
[71.04–73.24] How do we calculate an empirical formula?
[73.98–75.14] There are four steps involved.
[75.84–78.52] First, write down the mass of each element.
[79.02–84.46] Second, divide each mass by its relative atomic mass to calculate the number of moles.
[85.14–88.80] Third, divide all the mole values by the smallest value.
[89.26–93.74] Finally, use the simplest whole number ratio to write the empirical formula.
[94.22–96.22] Let's apply these steps to an example.
[96.94–100.44] Example 1. Calculating empirical formula from masses.
[100.90–108.62] A compound contains 2.4 grams of magnesium and 1.6 grams of oxygen. Calculate its empirical formula.
[109.12–114.32] The relative atomic masses are magnesium 24 and oxygen 16.
[114.82–118.84] First, divide the mass of magnesium by its relative atomic mass.
[119.32–123.64] 2.4 divided by 24 gives 0.1 moles.
[123.92–129.08] For oxygen, 1.6 divided by 16 also gives 0.1 moles.
[129.62–133.64] Next, divide both values by the smallest, which is 0.1.
[133.98–136.04] For magnesium, we obtain one.
[136.42–138.60] For oxygen, we also obtain one.
[139.10–141.86] Therefore the simplest ratio is one to one.
[142.28–144.90] The empirical formula is MgO.
[145.64–148.06] Practice question 1. Now it's your turn.
[148.54–161.42] A compound contains 4.6 grams of sodium and 1.6 grams of oxygen. The relative atomic masses are sodium 23 and oxygen 16. Calculate its empirical formula.
[161.96–165.20] Pause the video and try the question before we go through the answer.
[165.84–166.96] Let's check your answer.
[167.44–176.16] First, divide the mass of sodium by its relative atomic mass. 4.6 divided by 23 gives 0.2 moles.
[176.56–182.12] For oxygen, 1.6 divided by 16 gives 0.1 moles.
[182.78–187.06] Next, divide both values by the smallest, which is 0.1.
[187.44–192.06] For sodium, 0.2 divided by 0.1 gives two.
[192.40–197.14] For oxygen, 0.1 divided by 0.1 gives one.
[197.64–205.40] Therefore, the simplest ratio of sodium to oxygen is two to one. The empirical formula is Na2O.

[208.40–212.22] Example 2. Calculating empirical formula from percentages.
[212.98–224.72] Sometimes examination questions give us the percentage composition of a compound rather than the masses. The method is almost the same except that we first assume a total mass of 100 g.
[225.32–236.58] Let's consider an example. A compound contains 40% carbon, 6.7% hydrogen and 53.3% oxygen. Calculate its empirical formula.
[237.02–242.84] The relative atomic masses are carbon 12, hydrogen 1 and oxygen 16.
[243.34–253.84] First, assume we have 100 grams of the compound. This gives us 40 grams of carbon, 6.7 grams of hydrogen and 53.3 grams of oxygen.
[254.26–257.66] Now, divide each mass by its relative atomic mass.
[258.16–263.58] For carbon, 40 divided by 12 gives approximately 3.33 moles.
[264.04–268.78] For hydrogen, 6.7 divided by 1 gives 6.7 moles.
[269.16–275.58] For oxygen, 53.3 divided by 16 gives approximately 3.33 moles.
[276.08–280.96] Next, divide all three values by the smallest, approximately 3.33.
[281.48–289.18] This gives us a ratio of approximately 1 to 2 to 1. Therefore, the empirical formula is CH2O.
[289.68–295.64] Remember, when percentages are given, assume 100 g first, then follow the same steps.
[296.30–300.06] Practice question 2. Now, let's try our final question.
[300.52–317.14] A compound contains 52.2% carbon, 13.0% hydrogen and 34.8% oxygen. The relative atomic masses are carbon 12, hydrogen 1 and oxygen 16. Calculate its empirical formula.
[317.74–319.70] Pause the video and work through the calculation.
[320.40–332.70] Let's check your answer. First, assume we have 100 g of the compound. This gives us 52.2 g of carbon, 13 g of hydrogen and 34.8 g of oxygen.
[333.14–354.14] Next, divide each mass by its relative atomic mass. For carbon, 52.2 divided by 12 gives 4.35 moles. For hydrogen, 13 divided by 1 gives 13 moles. For oxygen, 34.8 divided by 16 gives 2.175 moles.
[354.82–368.56] Now, divide all three values by the smallest, which is 2.175. For carbon, we obtain two. For hydrogen, we obtain approximately six. For oxygen, we obtain one.
[369.38–378.02] Therefore, the simplest whole number ratio is two to six to one. The empirical formula is C2H6O.
