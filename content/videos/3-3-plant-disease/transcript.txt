Signs of disease include stunted growth, spots on leaves, areas of decay, growths, pests and discoloured or misshapen leaves. Rose black spot is a fungal disease that causes black spots on leaves which turn yellow and fall early. It spreads by wind and rain. Tobacco mosaic virus causes mosaic patterns on leaves and reduces photosynthesis. Nitrate deficiency causes stunted growth because nitrates are needed for protein. Magnesium deficiency causes chlorosis because magnesium is needed, to make chlorophyll. Plants defend themselves with physical barriers like waxy cuticles and thorns and chemical defences such as antibacterial chemicals. Mineral deficiencies can resemble infectious diseases, so the pattern of symptoms matters. Magnesium deficiency commonly causes intervenal chlorosis, particularly in older species because magnesium is a mobile nutrient and is required for chlorophyll. Nitrate deficiency can cause poor growth because nitrogen is needed to make amino acids and proteins. A plant's appearance is evidence, but careful diagnosis may require further investigation.
