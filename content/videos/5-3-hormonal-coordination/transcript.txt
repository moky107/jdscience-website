Hello and welcome back to JD Science. Today we are exploring hormones. Hormones are chemical messengers released by endocrine glands into the bloodstream. They are slower than nerves but last longer. The pancreas makes insulin, the adrenal gland makes adrenaline, the pituitary makes FSH and LH, the testes make testosterone, and the ovaries make estrogen and progesterone. The pituitary is the master gland. In type 1 diabetes, the pancreas does not make enough insulin and insulin is injected. In type 2, the body doesn't respond well to insulin and it is managed through diet and exercise. The menstrual cycle involves FSH, LH, estrogen and progesterone. FSH matures an egg, LH triggers ovulation and progesterone maintains the uterus lining. When water is low, ADH is released and the kidney reabsorbs more water. The endocrine system releases hormones into the bloodstream and only cells with suitable receptors respond. The pituitary coordinates several glands but hormone control involves feed loops rather than a single gland acting alone. Type 1 diabetes usually involves autoimmune destruction of insulin producing beta cells. Type 2 diabetes involves insulin resistance and may the also involve reduced insulin production. Treatment varies and may include medicines as well as lifestyle measures. As you revise this topic, try to explain the key process in your own words.
